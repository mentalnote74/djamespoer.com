From career documents to a job decision

The problem

A job asks for a capability; a resume contains a similar phrase. That overlap does not establish what the person did, how much responsibility they held, or whether they meet the requirement. A polished application can conceal those differences.

The intended product sequence

  1. Ingest evidence. Keep source documents and their provenance.
  2. Extract and normalize claims. Propose specific statements with source references, without broadening their meaning.
  3. Owner verifies or corrects. Accept accurate wording, correct overstatements, or reject unsupported suggestions.
  4. Build the active Library of Truth. Use current approved claims; retain superseded claims in history.
  5. Extract JD requirements. Separate the job's individual requirements instead of treating the description as a bag of keywords.
  6. Search the evidence. Find relevant approved claims and inspect the sources behind them.
  7. Classify each requirement. Return Proven, Supported, Confirmation Needed or Gap, with the evidence and limits behind that decision.
  8. Draft source-backed application output. Use supported wording, preserve source links and leave unresolved qualifications explicit.

This is the intended end-to-end sequence, not a claim that the full evaluator is running here. A Gap describes the evidence available, not proof that a person lacks a skill.

What the public demo actually does

The earlier phrase-overlap demo on the left compares three fixed topics with fictional text. It also reuses the real review controller for choosing, correcting or rejecting sample wording and inspecting its history. Everything resets with this tab.

It does not extract JD requirements, search a private Library of Truth, classify requirements, generate an application or call an AI service. Its matches are not qualification results. It illustrates why phrase matching is insufficient.

What the protected product supports today

The owner workflow supports private evidence intake, attributed source-linked proposals, explicit Accept / Correct / Reject decisions, active reviewed claims, correction history and explicit resolution of open questions. Review approval alone does not determine evidence strength or a job-requirement result.

Automatic claim extraction and normalization, JD requirement extraction, evidence search, the four result states and application generation are not wired into that owner workflow. They remain product direction. No protected evaluator entry is available from this page.

AI proposes; evidence and owner approval determine truth

In the intended evaluator, AI proposes claim wording, possible matches and drafts. Sources must support the wording, and the owner must explicitly approve or correct a claim before it enters the active Library of Truth. Rejecting a suggestion creates no approved claim. A later approved correction preserves the earlier acceptance in history.

A plausible AI answer cannot approve itself, resolve an open question automatically, or turn weak evidence into a Proven requirement. The system must ask for confirmation or show the gap instead of filling it with persuasive copy.
