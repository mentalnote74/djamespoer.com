## PRE-GENERATION STORY NOTE — IMAGE GENERATOR PRODUCTION BRIEF

**Status: APPROVED FOR V1 GENERATION — 2026-10-08**

### Generation discipline

Every generation is a clean-room first attempt. The image generator receives only the current production art brief. Previous outputs, critiques, failures, and correction history are not supplied unless a later experiment deliberately introduces a specific cultural reference. Failed output changes the specification, not the generator's memory.

### Production canvas and composition

Create an **extremely dense, colorful, highly detailed original 2D animated-cartoon panoramic town mural** packed edge-to-edge with simultaneous miniature stories, characters, environmental details, hidden visual jokes, and discoveries at multiple depths.

The finished image must behave like a **seek-and-find puzzle illustration**. Populate foreground, midground, background, streets, sidewalks, windows, rooftops, alleys, vehicles, businesses, and skyline with dozens of small activities. No single character should dominate the composition. The viewer should instinctively scan repeatedly and continue discovering new things.

**Nothing gets explained that can be discovered.**

The scene should initially read as wonderfully chaotic creative/engineering madness. Closer inspection reveals individual jokes and relationships.

**Canvas: 1280 × 194 px.** Compose specifically for this extremely wide, thin panoramic format. Use perspective, overlapping structures, rooflines, streets, and scale to create depth while distributing discoveries across the full horizontal canvas. Do not compose a conventional landscape intended to be cropped later.

### The Town of Creative

This town is named **Creative**.

Place a large old-school municipal water tower deeper in the skyline with clearly readable text:

**CREATIVE**

The water tower naturally identifies the town and functions as the mural's title without a separate title treatment. No establishment year is included in V1.

### Silent cultural background discoveries

Fill every available region of Creative with silent discoveries. **The more, the better.**

Beyond the required autobiographical scenes, populate the town with dozens of tiny unexplained visual micro-stories, strange characters, cultural echoes, cryptids, odd objects, background incidents, suspicious coincidences, and absurd interactions.

Some should be immediately funny. Some should require zooming. Some may not be noticed until repeated viewings. Familiar-looking celebrities, legendary figures, movie and television archetypes, historical characters, monsters, aliens, cryptids, and figures from the shared cultural zeitgeist may simply appear among ordinary townspeople without introduction.

Nobody points them out. Nobody explains them. No labels, dialogue, captions, arrows, or sound effects identify them.

Examples of the intended subtle discovery behavior include:

* a suspiciously Elvis-like citizen casually going about ordinary town business;
* a tiny green visitor naturally waiting somewhere among ordinary citizens;
* Bigfoot casually crossing a distant alley or street;
* oddly serious medieval knights appearing around town;
* armored travelers moving through town on foot while carrying coconuts, with no horses and no explanation;
* a small white rabbit sitting completely still in an ordinary location with an unexpectedly vicious expression;
* familiar-looking characters from different cartoon eras casually sharing an ordinary board game at a sidewalk table;
* familiar-looking mystery-solving companions naturally waiting in line at a sandwich shop.

Characters who seemingly have no reason to occupy the same universe may casually share ordinary moments together: playing games, waiting for food, riding the bus, arguing over parking, standing in Customer Service, or simply passing one another on the street.

These examples establish permission and visual behavior, not a complete inventory. **Invent many additional discoveries that are not specified here.** Keep them small, naturally integrated into the environment, and subordinate to the required Creative-town stories.

The viewer should never be confident they have found everything.

### Visual hierarchy

The astronaut and balloon, CREATIVE water tower, Larry, Mission Control, Rubber Duck Customer Service, bus station, bike shed, saloon, and yak-shaving scene should anchor different regions of the panorama **without turning any of them into a giant isolated centerpiece**.

The white van, D&R pizza delivery, monitor sticker, yak disclaimer, additional yaks, pedestrians, vehicles, windows, businesses, cultural discoveries, and miscellaneous town activity create secondary and tertiary discoveries.

Fill otherwise unused spaces with believable miniature town life and harmless visual absurdity so the scene remains rewarding under prolonged inspection.

### Style

Original 2D animated-cartoon illustration with expressive linework, exaggerated characters, readable silhouettes, rich environmental detail, lively visual storytelling, and strong spatial depth.

Playful, irreverent, intelligent, professionally art-directed, densely populated, and intentionally designed to reward zooming and repeated inspection.

This is a visual autobiography, software-culture satire, portfolio demonstration, and treasure hunt occupying the same world.

**It is explained nowhere, but it explains everything.**

And somewhere in the finished mural, there should always be another yak to discover.
